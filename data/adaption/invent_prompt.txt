# Invent a Dataset — Description Prompt for Adaption Labs
# Used in Adaption "Invent a Dataset" tool to generate synthetic fiction continuity training examples.
# Contract reference: contracts/schema.json

Generate supervised training examples for a story continuity analyst.

Each example consists of:
1. Input:
   - A 300 to 800 word fiction chapter excerpt
   - A list of known characters so far
   - The chapter number
2. Output:
   - A structured JSON object matching the NarrativeOS contract schema containing:
     - "characters": new characters introduced, plus aliases for known characters.
     - "facts": story-relevant claims. Emphasize:
       * Physical and numerical attributes: age (e.g., "sixty-two years old"), eye color, hair color, scars, and identifying traits.
       * Character state: confirmed death, illness, injuries, or absence.
       * Temporal and travel assertions: stated travel durations (e.g., "fourteen hours to drive"), distances, and alibis.
       * Core plot facts: secrets, crimes, wills, and relationships.
     - "knowledge_events": when a character LEARNS a fact in this chapter, with mode ("witnessed", "told", "overheard", "deduced") and source character ID (or null).
     - "references": when a character speaks about, references, or acts on a fact in this chapter.
     - "events": key narrative occurrences. Emphasize:
       * "time_marker": extract explicit temporal markers whenever present (e.g., "Day 3 evening", "half past seven on Wednesday", "Friday morning", "after midnight"). Do NOT omit stated timestamps.
       * "location": exact room, building, or city (e.g., "sitting room library", "Blackwood Manor morning room", "London flat", "Edinburgh").
       * "participants": complete list of character IDs present in the scene, especially tracking arrivals and departures.

Strict Invariants & Rules:
- Output must be strict, valid JSON without markdown formatting or commentary.
- Every item ("fact", "knowledge_event", "reference", "event") MUST have a "quote" copied EXACTLY from the chapter excerpt, character-for-character, 5-25 words long.
- Do NOT infer what is not on the page. If unsure, omit the item.
- Do NOT invent facts or time markers not explicitly stated in the excerpt.
- Cover varied genres: mystery, fantasy, thriller, historical drama.
- Explicitly include challenging edge cases addressing known continuity failure modes:
  1. Timeline & Travel: scenes with explicit travel times, departure/arrival timestamps, and evening/morning markers.
  2. Presence & Colocation: scenes tracking who entered, who departed, who stayed in a room, and scenes confirming a character's death.
  3. Attribute Drift: explicit mentions of character age, eye color, or physical descriptions.
  4. Negative Examples: excerpts where no new facts or knowledge events occur (sparse extraction).
